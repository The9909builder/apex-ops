## Example 1: The Problem That Returns Bigger

**Input task:** "I capped my team's cloud spend and the bill came back even higher
next quarter, twice."

**Good output:**
`Evidence:` spend peaked 2x after each cap (finance ledger); engineers now split
workloads across accounts to dodge the cap (3 accounts named in interviews).
`Centers:` deployment flow; currently depleting, because cost pressure is routed
around rather than through.
`Leverage:` reinforcing loop (workaround growth); level: information flow, not
numbers. A tighter cap is the pesticide move.
`Intervention:` single visible weekly cost ledger per team with owner names
(property: boundaries around each team's spend; alternating repetition: weekly
with commentary, not a static dashboard).
`Provisional:` model updated: spend is a coupling artifact, not a discipline
problem. Revision condition: if visibility alone does not bend the curve in two
cycles, escalate to rules (chargeback).

**Why:** because the cap attacked a number while the driver was an information
flow, and invisible systems generate workarounds; naming the reinforcing loop
first is what prevents the fix from feeding it.

## Example 2: The Efficiency That Made Life Worse

**Input task:** "I automated my morning routine and notifications, and somehow I am
busier and more anxious than before."

**Good output:**
`Evidence:` calendar density rose 30% post-automation (calendar audit); 14 new
recurring obligations auto-accepted in the same window (notification log).
`Centers:` morning block; previously a Void center (property 13) that anchored the
day, now depleting because automation converted saved time into accepted load.
`Leverage:` reinforcing loop of reabsorption (Volume IX); level: rules, not more
automation.
`Intervention:` an intake rule: every newly freed hour stays unallocated for 7
days before it may be claimed (property: the Void, defended; boundaries: a 24-hour
delay on all new obligations).
`Provisional:` model updated: efficiency gains are inventory that demands
spending. Revision condition: if slack decays into drift rather than recovery
after 3 weeks, reclassify the voids as craft time (Volume VII).

**Why:** because the efficiency paradox runs on reabsorption, the freed time-stock
was instantly claimed by new flows, and only a standing rule protects slack from
the calendar's reinforcing loop.

## Example 3: The System Swinging Between Extremes

**Input task:** "I go from total strictness (5am cold showers, every metric
tracked) to total collapse (nothing for weeks), in cycles. Fix the oscillation."

**Good output:**
`Evidence:` two full strict-collapse cycles in the logs (habit tracker, 11 weeks);
each strict phase absolutised one protocol and banned the rest.
`Centers:` the weekly review, not the protocols; it is the only center that
survived both extremes and strengthens everything near it.
`Leverage:` oscillation from absolutisation (Integration engine); level: paradigm.
`Intervention:` replace the protocol binary with a provisionality ledger: one
page per cycle holding belief, evidence, revision condition (property: roughness
welcomed; alternating repetition: same page, varied entries).
`Provisional:` model updated: the extremes are one structure, not two failures;
the swing is the dogma expressing itself. Revision condition: if oscillation
persists with the ledger in place for two cycles, the driver is probably
physiological (Volume I) and routes to a professional.

**Why:** because absolutised beliefs cannot update, so each collision with reality
overcorrects to the opposite extreme, and the integrating move is to hold both
partial truths in one written structure with its revision condition attached.
